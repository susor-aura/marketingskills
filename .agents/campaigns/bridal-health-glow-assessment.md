# Bridal Health & Glow Assessment
**Typeform Onboarding Survey · NÉVUE Bridal IV Concierge**  
**Campaign:** Waitlist qualification + personalization intake  
**Platform:** Typeform (one question per screen)  
**Target completion time:** 2–3 minutes  
**Tone:** Luxury concierge — warm, specific, editorial. Never clinical.  
**Created:** 2026-04-21

---

## Strategy Notes

- **Typeform format chosen** because one-question-per-screen feels like a private consultation, not a medical intake form. The format itself signals luxury.
- **Question order builds commitment**: easy/fun details first → aspirational goals → medical screening (by then they've invested too much to bail) → the emotional open-text question → contact info last.
- **Progress bar on** — set expectations. Never hide how much is left.
- **All medical questions framed as "about you"** — not "medical history." Clinical language kills the experience.
- **Submit CTA = outcome language** — "Reserve My Consultation" not "Submit."
- **Skip logic** used in two places to keep the form relevant (noted below).

## Section 2 — Physical Goals

*Purpose: aspirational, fun, on-brand. Brides love answering these. This is where Typeform's conversational format shines.*

### Q6 — How You Want to Feel (multi-select, required)

**Question:**
> On the morning of your wedding — which of these feel most essential?

**Help text:**
> Select any that matter. There are no wrong answers.

**Options (select all that apply):**
- Radiant skin that reads on camera
- Calm, steady hands — no shaky nerves
- Energy that lasts through the reception
- Completely hydrated, head to toe
- Recovered from a late rehearsal dinner
- Clear, puffiness-free from travel
- Focused and fully present

**CRO rationale:**
- Benefits-over-features language throughout
- "Reads on camera" is specific, vivid, and a pain point brides actually worry about
- Each option implies an outcome NÉVUE can deliver — the bride is essentially self-selecting her formula

---

### Q7 — Priority (opinion scale, required)

**Question:**
> If you could only feel *one* thing perfectly on your wedding day — would it lean more toward calm, or toward glow?

**Scale:** 1 to 7

**Labels:**
- 1 — Pure calm
- 4 — Both equally
- 7 — Pure glow

**CRO rationale:** Forces a preference → helps us recommend the right drip (ZenDrip vs. Radiance formula). Framed as aspiration, not diagnostic.

## Section 3 — Health Check

*Purpose: capture medical screening without sounding like a medical form. Every question is framed from her perspective, not ours.*

### Q8 — Previous IV Experience (multiple choice, required)

**Question:**
> Have you ever received IV therapy before?

**Options:**
- Yes — I'm a regular
- Yes — once or twice
- No — this would be my first time
- I'm still deciding if it's right for me

**CRO rationale:** "Still deciding" is an escape valve that keeps nervous brides in the funnel. Never force a yes/no when ambivalence is the real answer.

---

### Q9 — Comfort with Needles (multiple choice, required)

**Question:**
> How would you describe your comfort with needles?

**Options:**
- Completely fine with them
- Mostly fine — slightly uneasy
- Anxious, but willing
- Genuinely needle-averse — this is my biggest concern

**CRO rationale:** The fourth option gives us our most valuable signal. Any bride who picks it becomes a priority call — she is our textbook vein-mapping case. Never treat this as disqualification.

---

### Q10 — Allergies, Sensitivities & Medications (long text, required)

**Question:**
> Is there anything we should know about your body?

**Help text:**
> Allergies, sensitivities, current medications, recent surgeries, or anything your doctor has flagged. Even small things — your nurse will thank you.

**Placeholder:**
> Example: seasonal allergies, take vitamin D daily, slight bruising tendency.

**CRO rationale:**
- "Your body" instead of "your medical history" — softer, more personal
- The placeholder gives permission to over-share small details
- "Your nurse will thank you" transforms a chore into a favor

---

### Q11 — Pregnancy Screen (yes / no / prefer not to say, required)

**Question:**
> Are you currently pregnant or think you may be?

**Options:**
- No
- Yes
- I'd rather discuss privately

**CRO rationale:** "I'd rather discuss privately" is essential. Some brides will be in IVF, early pregnancy, or simply not want to disclose through a form. Protects trust. Any non-"no" response triggers a direct call before booking.

---

### Q12 — Energy Baseline (opinion scale, optional)

**Question:**
> How would you describe your energy on a typical week?

**Scale:** 1 to 5

**Labels:**
- 1 — Running on fumes
- 5 — Fully charged

**CRO rationale:** Low-stakes question that helps us calibrate formula strength. Framed as self-awareness, not diagnosis. Optional because it's not critical.

---

## Section 4 — Biggest Wedding Day Fear

*Purpose: the emotional core. This answer is pure gold — it tells the follow-up call exactly what to lead with.*

### Q13 — The Fear (long text, required)

**Eyebrow:** `The real question.`

**Question:**
> What keeps you up at night about your wedding day?

**Help text:**
> There's no such thing as a silly answer. Whatever you tell us here will shape how your nurse cares for you.

**Placeholder:**
> Example: I cry when I'm nervous and don't want swollen eyes in the photos.

**CRO rationale:**
- "The real question" as eyebrow signals this is the important one — raises engagement
- "Keeps you up at night" is specific, evocative, far better than "biggest concern"
- The placeholder is a real, vulnerable example — gives permission to be honest
- "Will shape how your nurse cares for you" reframes the question as personalization, not interrogation
- This answer will be the single most valuable input for the sales call

---

## Section 5 — Contact

*Purpose: saved for last. By now she's answered vulnerable questions — email/phone feels natural, not gated.*

### Q14 — Email (email, required)

**Question:**
> Where should we send your personalized assessment?

**Placeholder:**
> you@email.com

**CRO rationale:**
- "Where should we send your *personalized assessment*" = what she gets, not what we want
- Implies follow-up deliverable = worth the email

---

### Q15 — Phone (phone, optional)

**Question:**
> And a number, for the nurse on your day?

**Help text:**
> Optional. We'll only text you when it matters — confirmation, your dedicated nurse's contact, and a quiet check-in the week of the wedding.

**Placeholder:**
> (727) 555-0100

**CRO rationale:**
- "For the nurse on your day" answers the unspoken "why do you need my number" question
- The help text promises exactly what will happen — no spam anxiety
- "A quiet check-in" is on-brand; most brands would say "important updates"

## 16 — Thank You Screen

**Eyebrow:**
> Received.

**Title:**
> We're reviewing your assessment.

**Description:**
> Within one business day, one of our concierge team members will reach out personally with your recommended program and next steps. No generic forms, no automated sequences — just a note, from us to you.
>
> In the meantime, if you'd like to read about what to expect on the day, we've put together a short guide.

**Primary button:**
> Read: What to Expect →

**Secondary button (lower, lighter weight):**
> Return to NÉVUE

**CRO rationale:**
- "Received." is quieter and more confident than "Thank you!" — signals luxury
- Sets a specific expectation (one business day) so she's not wondering
- "No generic forms, no automated sequences" addresses the unspoken fear that she just became another lead in a drip sequence
- Secondary action (read the guide) keeps her engaged while she waits

## Field-Level Data Use

For the sales / nurse handoff, the highest-value fields are:

| Field | Use in follow-up |
|---|---|
| Q6 (How she wants to feel) | Recommend formula — ZenDrip vs. Radiance vs. Hydration |
| Q7 (Calm vs. Glow scale) | Confirm formula lean |
| Q9 (Needle comfort) | Flag any "needle-averse" bride for priority vein-mapping demo |
| Q10 (Body notes) | Pre-call review by licensed nurse before booking |
| Q11 (Pregnancy) | Any non-"no" → direct phone call before any booking |
| Q13 (The fear) | **Open the sales call by addressing this verbatim** — e.g., "Before anything else — you mentioned you cry when you're nervous. Let me tell you exactly how the morning will go." |

## Design Specifications

**Typeform settings to apply:**

- **Theme:** Custom — Névue Linen background `#F5EDD8`, Névue Navy buttons `#1B2744`, Névue Gold accents `#C9A96E`
- **Font:** Cormorant Garamond for question titles, Inter for help text / options
- **Progress bar:** On. Visible at bottom.
- **Animation speed:** Slow. Luxury = unhurried.
- **Navigation:** Allow back button. Never trap.
- **Required field indicator:** No asterisks. Use a subtle "Required" label if needed.
- **Keyboard shortcuts:** On (enter to advance feels premium).

**Mobile optimization:**
- Autofill enabled on email/phone
- Tel keyboard for phone field
- Email keyboard for email field
- One question per screen is inherently mobile-friendly

## A/B Test Ideas (post-launch)

| Test | Hypothesis |
|---|---|
| Welcome copy: "Private Consultation" vs. "Begin Your Assessment" | Consultation framing increases completion |
| Q13 placeholder: vulnerable example vs. neutral example | Vulnerable example increases answer depth |
| Phone: required vs. optional | Optional will increase completion but reduce qualified lead rate — measure which wins economically |
| Thank you screen: book-a-call CTA vs. content-read CTA | Does adding a direct booking link improve conversion to call? |

## Expected Metrics

- **Completion rate target:** 70%+ (industry avg for bridal lead forms ~45–55%; one-question-per-screen typically adds 15–20 points)
- **Time to complete target:** under 3 minutes median
- **Drop-off hotspots to watch:** Q10 (long text — body notes) and Q11 (pregnancy). If drop-off spikes here, soften further.
